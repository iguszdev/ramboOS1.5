Writing your own app for RamboOS
==================================

RamboOS has a real "run" command that loads and executes a program
you write yourself, from its own reserved slot on the disk image.
This isn't a simulation - it's actual machine code, assembled by you,
loaded by the bootloader's kernel via a real BIOS disk read, and
executed with a real x86 far call.

HOW IT WORKS
------------
- The disk image reserves 20 sectors (10KB) starting right after the
  kernel, at LBA 41, for exactly one app.
- When you type "run" in RamboOS, the kernel reads those 20 sectors
  into memory at segment 0x1000, offset 0, and does:
      call 0x1000:0x0000
  Your app runs from there with full access to BIOS interrupts
  (screen, keyboard, disk, timer, etc - anything a real-mode program
  can normally use).
- When your app is done, it MUST return with a plain "retf" (far
  return) instruction. That hands control cleanly back to RamboOS,
  which then keeps running normally.

WRITING AN APP
--------------
1. Write your program in x86 assembly (NASM syntax), 16-bit real mode,
   assembled as a flat binary with ORG 0x0000 (see demo-app.asm for a
   full working example).

2. At the very start of your app, set up your own DS/ES, since
   RamboOS's segment registers aren't guaranteed to match yours:

       mov ax, cs
       mov ds, ax
       mov es, ax

3. Do whatever you want - print things, read the keyboard, beep the
   speaker, read the clock, etc. Stick to BIOS interrupts (int 0x10,
   0x16, 0x1A, 0x13...) rather than touching hardware directly unless
   you know what you're doing.

4. End your program with:

       retf

   Don't use "int 0x20" or "hlt" to exit - use retf, since your app
   was entered with a far CALL and RamboOS expects a matching return.

5. Keep your app under 10240 bytes (20 sectors). demo-app.asm is a
   good template - just copy it and start editing.

ASSEMBLING AND INSTALLING YOUR APP
-----------------------------------
1. Assemble it to a flat binary:

       nasm -f bin myapp.asm -o myapp.bin

2. Write it into the app slot of ramboos.img, which starts at byte
   offset 41 * 512 = 20992:

       dd if=myapp.bin of=ramboos.img conv=notrunc bs=512 seek=41

   (conv=notrunc is important - without it, dd will truncate the
   rest of the disk image!)

3. Boot ramboos.img and type "run". Your app runs.

That's it - there's no compiler, no SDK, no permission system. If you
can write x86 assembly, RamboOS will run it. That's kind of the whole
point: it's a real, small, honest little OS, and now it's yours to
extend too.
